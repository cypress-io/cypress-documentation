# Manage Teams and Project Access

**A Cypress Cloud team is a reusable group of users that is granted access to a specific
set of projects.** A team sets project access once, at the team level, instead of
project by project, user by user.

Teams shape your organization so that every person sees the projects relevant to
their work, and nothing else.

## Why use teams

As your organization grows, a single shared view of every project becomes harder to
navigate and harder to govern. Teams solve this by aligning access in Cypress Cloud with
how your organization actually works. The most valuable outcomes teams deliver are:

- **A focused, clutter-free experience.** Each person sees only the projects their team
  needs, which makes navigating Cypress Cloud faster and reduces the chance of acting on
  the wrong project.
- **Clean separation for shared organizations.** When multiple business units or
  subsidiaries share one Cypress Cloud organization, teams keep each unit's projects
  cleanly separated while preserving a single billing relationship.
- **Privacy and security by default.** You can restrict sensitive projects to the
  specific people who should see them, so access is intentional rather than open to
  everyone.
- **Distributed administration.** The dedicated _team admin_ role puts day-to-day team
  members in charge of their own projects, removing bottlenecks and freeing organization
  owners and admins from routine access requests.
- **Usage insight by team.** Because Cypress Cloud organizes access by team, you can see how your organization uses Cypress in
  [Enterprise Reporting](/cloud/features/analytics/enterprise-reporting). Team-level usage insight is useful
  for chargebacks, capacity planning, and measuring adoption.

:::caution
[Public projects](https://on.cypress.io/what-is-project-access) are visible to all Cypress Cloud users, including those outside of your organization, regardless of team membership.
:::

## Manage teams

Teams are flexible by design, so you can model them to fit your organization without
running into artificial limits or rigid rules. Organization
[owner or admin](/cloud/account-management/users#User-roles) roles can create teams in
Cypress Cloud. These organization owners and admins, along with a role called the
_team admin_, are then able to configure the team as required.

This flexibility is what makes teams valuable at any scale. You can start small and
expand your structure as your organization grows:

- There is no limit on the number of teams you can set up for your organization.
- All users that are members of the organization are members of the default team named _Everyone_.
- You can assign a user to multiple teams.
- Roles are defined at the user level. That user's role determines the permissions granted
  within the team.
- There is no limit on the number of projects you can assign to a team.
- You can assign the same project to multiple teams.
- Team-based project access only controls what users can view in Cypress Cloud. Team-based project access does not affect the ability to record runs to a project from the Cypress App.

### Create a team

Only organization [owner or admin](/cloud/account-management/users#User-roles) roles can create
teams in Cypress Cloud. Other roles do not see the button to create a new team.

1. On the Users & Teams page, select the **Teams** tab
2. Click **+ Create team**

   <DocsImage
     src="/img/cloud/teams/create-new-team.png"
     alt="Navigate to the Users & Teams page in Cypress Cloud and select the button to create a new team."
   />

3. On the _Create new team_ modal, enter the team name and click **Save**

### Assign projects to a team

Project assignment is the heart of what makes teams valuable: it is the control that
decides exactly which projects a team's members can see and work with. Assigning the
right projects keeps each team focused on what matters to them and keeps everything else
out of view.

Once you create a team, it is important to assign projects to the team. If a team has
no projects assigned to it, then a user whose only team is that team does not have access to
any projects in Cypress Cloud.

<DocsImage
  src="/img/cloud/teams/assign-projects-to-team.png"
  alt="Click the + icon in the Project access column to assign projects to a Cypress Cloud team."
/>

On the **Teams** tab, assign projects to a team by clicking the **+** icon in the
**Project access** column. The modal that appears has two options:

1. **Assign all projects to team**

   Use this option if you have a team that should access all projects, including projects that
   already exist and ones you create in the future. **Assign all projects to team** is the default selection for
   the **Everyone** team.

   <DocsImage
     src="/img/cloud/teams/assign-projects-to-team-modal-all.png"
     alt="Grant team project access to all existing and future projects when assigning projects to a team."
     width="70%"
   />

2. **Assign selected projects to team**

   Use this option if you want to assign specific projects to a team. Search for projects by name
   and select (or unselect) them to assign projects to the team.

   <DocsImage
     src="/img/cloud/teams/assign-projects-to-team-modal-selected.png"
     alt="Select individual projects from a list to control a team's project access in Cypress Cloud."
     width="70%"
   />

### Assign users to a team

Adding users to a team is how access translates into everyday productivity: members
immediately gain the right view of Cypress Cloud, and you grant that access once at the
team level rather than repeating it for every project. Cypress Cloud manages
membership from two places, so you can work from whichever view fits the task
at hand.

1. On the **Teams** tab, select users for a team by clicking the **+** icon in the **Members** column

   <DocsImage
     src="/img/cloud/teams/assign-members-to-team.png"
     alt="Click the + icon in the Members column to assign Cypress Cloud users to a team."
   />

   The **Members** column adds multiple users to a team at the same time. Browse the user list
   or search by name, and then select (or unselect) them to assign users as members of this team.

   <DocsImage
     src="/img/cloud/teams/assign-members-to-team-modal.png"
     alt="Select individual users from a list to add them as members of a Cypress Cloud team."
     width="70%"
   />

2. On the **Users** tab, select teams for a user by clicking the **+** icon in the **Teams** column

   <DocsImage
     src="/img/cloud/teams/assign-team-to-user.png"
     alt="Click the + icon in the Teams column to assign a team to a specific Cypress Cloud user."
   />

   On the edit modal, you can search by team name and then select (or unselect) them to assign the user to
   additional teams.

   <DocsImage
     src="/img/cloud/teams/assign-team-to-user-modal.png"
     alt="Select individual teams from a list to assign them to a specific Cypress Cloud user."
     width="60%"
   />

:::tip

The label 'No project access' appears if you haven't yet given a team access to any
projects. The label helps you avoid mistakes when assigning users to teams.

:::

### Delete a team

To delete a team, click on the **X** icon at the right side of the team's row.
Cypress Cloud prompts you for confirmation before deleting the team.

<DocsImage
  src="/img/cloud/teams/delete-team.png"
  alt="Click the X icon in the team's row to delete a Cypress Cloud team."
/>

## Example team structures

There is no single correct way to organize teams. The right shape depends on how your
organization is structured and what you're trying to protect. The examples below show
realistic end states that organizations commonly land on.

### Product squads

A single engineering organization where each squad owns a set of repositories. Each squad sees only the projects it owns, while the Admins team retains full visibility for
leadership and platform-wide debugging.

| Team     | Members                       | Project access                |
| -------- | ----------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Admins   | Owners and Admins             | All projects                  |
| Checkout | Checkout squad engineers + QA | Cart, Payments, Order History |
| Search   | Search squad engineers + QA   | Search API, Search UI         |
| Platform | Platform/infra engineers      | Design System, CI Tooling     |

### Multiple business units sharing one organization

A parent company or agency where several business units (or clients) share one Cypress Cloud
organization but should not see each other's projects. Giving each unit its own team keeps that unit's work cleanly separated while preserving a single billing relationship.

| Team    | Members           | Project access           |
| ------- | ----------------- | ------------------------ |
| Admins  | Owners and Admins | All projects             |
| Brand A | Brand A team      | Brand A web, Brand A app |
| Brand B | Brand B team      | Brand B web, Brand B app |

### Restricted access for sensitive projects

A mostly-open organization that needs to lock down a small number of sensitive projects. Here the organization keeps the broad **Everyone** team for general projects, and a small, explicitly-managed
team gates the sensitive ones.

| Team     | Members                       | Project access             |
| -------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Everyone | All organization members      | All non-sensitive projects |
| Payments | Payments engineers + auditors | Payments, Billing          |
